About

People who know me for my art are usually surprised by how much I know about tech. People who know me through tech are surprised I’m an artist. I got both from my family.

My mom is an artist, and I grew up watching her work. My grandfather worked in IBM’s “computer department” in the ’60s. My dad worked in telecom, networking, and security, so we always had a computer in the house. I was the kid making banners in The Print Shop software and printing them on the dot matrix printer, hanging out on IRC, writing programs in QBasic, and gaming way past bedtime.

My other grandfather was a draftsman. I never met him, but years after he died, my family found his drafting tools in my grandmother’s attic. They’re in the original black case with purple velvet lining. Inside are compasses, dividers, and lead holders that still have the original lead from the ’50s. (Probably shouldn’t eat that.) I use them for my house portraits, at an old drafting table from the same era that I resurfaced myself.

A house portrait starts technical. I work out the scale and use the dividers to get the proportions right. Then comes the part I love: inking the details that make each house its own, plus a little whimsy.

My watercolor florals aim for a sort of scientific botanical style. My stickers start as hand drawings, and I finish them in Affinity or Illustrator. My Philly peanut butter cup started as an acrylic marker drawing and ended up as a clean vector.

Art also calms my mind. I’ve dealt with mental health stuff my whole life. When I’m drawing, I don’t think about anything else. I can work on one piece for hours, sometimes long enough that I forget to eat.

I’ve shown my work in Cheltenham Art Center’s juried show in 2025 and 2026. I’m also in Abington Art Center’s Small Works show this November. And I’ve had two featured artists shows at the Upper Dublin Public Library with my mom. Our styles are completely different, and I’m proud to hang my work next to hers.
